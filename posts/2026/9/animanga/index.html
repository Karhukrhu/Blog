My Relationship With Animanga

September 5, 2026

Soo... Maybe some of you have already noticed if you have been browsing through my art gallery, I'm one of those few among indiewebbers who doesn't do animanga at all. I'll try to expain that in a while, even though there's nothing to explain really tbh.

Currently, I'm 34 years old. That makes me a member of the generation that witnessed the huge rise of Japanese culture in their teens. I mean, I live in "remote" Europe so that's my point of view in this post. At that time, the Korean and Chinese cultures were crushed by Japanese when talking about Asia (at least in my country). Everything was about Japan pretty much. The fashion, the language, national culture and pop culture.

Suddenly, Japanese stuff was everywhere. The coolest kids even read their manga in Japanese. There were courses to draw Japanese styled characters, anime was a huge thing. You earned street-credibility by only reading manga that was printed like in Japan - from right to the left. There were accepted manga publishers and those of "poor quality". It was a subtantial thing among more or less alternative community in my youth. There were even special Japanese markers to colour your art. If you used them, you were truly a cool person.

I tried to keep up with the trend. I took a manga drawing course, bought manga from the neighbourhood supermarket and fell for lolita style. My dream was to acquire an edition of Gothic & Lolita Bible, but I never did.

I think I was 13 yrs old when I got my first lolita dress that I bought after creating a Paypal account without my parents knowing. That was my first ever purchase from abroad. It was a back and white tartan dress, but I can't find a picture of it anymore. If you refer to all the strict measurement guidelines for lolita dresses, this was way too short for me :'')

At the same time I was leaning towards gothic style and music which eventually took over the lolita one. But, I can still say, I have seen Mana live! Moi Dix Mois came to my city right when I was in the middle of this lolita phase. Anyway, I began to move away from Japanese culture in my late teens. I think it was actually never my thing, I just went for it because everyone else did. I never really enjoyed manga or anime with exception of few anime shows, Hayao Miyazaki ones.

Don't get me wrong though, I think the aestetics of animanga can be beautiful and appealing with all it's intricacy and carefully thought out animation. I just have a huge problem with the stories. I have to say, I've never read a good manga! The most books of manga I have is from the much appraised series of Death Note. My brain just doesn't sync with the story. Eventually, I bought the books just to collect them all, not because I enjoyed it, really.

At the latest, the final deathblow to my enthusiasm for animanga and Japanese culture happened when I started upper secondary school of visual arts. The art teachers there explicitly forbit us to draw manga/anime. They had seen enough of it I guess. I went for it. Well, actually I hadn't drawn manga since the seventh grade anyway.

All in all, I have seen very few animes. Most of them I haven't liked. I also have read very few mangas, I didn't enjoy any of the stories. I guess I'm just not the target audience. The point is that I tend to toss out things I don't enjoy even though I haven't tried them more than a couple of times. That's my problem, but I stick with my problem. Why spend time doing something you likely won't enjoy?

If you have any incredible mangas or animes to recommend me, I'm happy to hear! I want to get rid of this anti-animanga attitude I have now :')
